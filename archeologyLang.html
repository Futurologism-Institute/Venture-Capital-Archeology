A Dictionary and Grammar for Mathematical Archeology This is an invented formal language, not an established field. It treats archaeological reasoning as something you can write as precise sentences, with the evidence status of every claim built into the grammar. The vocabulary comes from our themes: the Green Sahara, Loropeni, the Kassena, and Narmer. Part 1: Grammar Sentence pattern Every sentence follows this order: [Place] · [Depth] · [Subject] · [Operator] · [Object] · [Evidence mark] Example: Loropeni · d2 · Kasen · links · Sahar-trade · -mi Meaning: "At Loropeni, at depth 2, the lineage links to the Saharan trade (inferred)." Word classes Class Role Marking Sites Fixed locations Capitalized (Loropeni, Gobero) Sets Groups of artifacts, people, or cultures Lowercase noun Functions Quantities that change over time End in -f Operators Verbs that act on sets Placed between subject and object Marks Evidence status Suffix on the final word Evidence marks (the core rule) You cannot finish a sentence without stating how you know it: Suffix Meaning Example source -ta Directly observed or excavated A dated burial at Gobero -mi Inferred from data Shared pottery style implies contact -ko Carried knowledge: oral tradition or community memory Saharan peoples' memory of a greener land -sa Speculative hypothesis, untested Oil in the Volta Basin Carried knowledge (-ko) is a first-class category. It is a data source to be tested against excavation, not set aside. A claim can be upgraded from -ko to -ta when a dig confirms it. Depth as tense Depth replaces past, present, and future. Prefix d plus a number counts strata downward (d0 is the surface, d3 is three layers down). The prefix x- marks unexcavated ground: x-d2 means "predicted layer 2, not yet dug." Operators Operator Symbol Meaning narm A ⊕ B Unify two sets into one while keeping both traces links A ~ B Evidence of contact between sets descends A → B Directed ancestry, B from A drains A ↘ B A set shrinks as another grows (e.g., lake to desert) encloses A ⊂ B Bounded containment tests A ? B A dig or survey is proposed to check B Numbers and uncertainty Dates carry their range with them: 5k±1k means 5,000 years with a spread of 1,000. Confidence is written c followed by a value from 0 to 1. A -sa claim above c0.5 is a grammatical error, because speculation cannot claim majority confidence without evidence. Part 2: Dictionary Nouns Sahar (set): the whole Saharan population and material culture across time. Loropen (n.): a bounded stone enclosure with a measurable perimeter and area. Loropen(11000 m²) is the Loropeni walls. Kasen (n.): a lineage chain. A Kasen-chain is a descent sequence with gaps marked, because real genealogies and migrations have missing links. Gobero-set (n.): a burial assemblage tied to a lake shoreline, named for the Niger site. Nabt (n.): a ritual or astronomical alignment feature, from Nabta Playa. Seriq (n.): a royal name-mark that proves political reach; from the serekh found at distant sites. Gap (n.): the unexcavated fraction of a region, between 0 and 1. Gap(Loropeni region) ≈ 0.95 would mean 95% unexcavated. Basin (n.): a sediment container that might hold hydrocarbons. Writes with a potential value, Basin(Volta, c0.2-sa). Functions Humf: the humidity function over time. Humf(Sahar) is high from about 14,500 to 5,000 years ago, then falls. Driff: the rate of southward population movement as Humf drops. Trad-f: trade flow, such as gold moving along trans-Saharan routes. Chronf: the dating uncertainty of any find. Verbs and operators narm (v.): to unify. From King Narmer, who is credited with joining Upper and Lower Egypt. Writes as ⊕. A narm leaves both original sets recoverable, so a unified culture is never treated as a blank slate. drift (v.): to move as a population under environmental pressure. Sahar drift South describes the post-humid migration. dig (v.): to sample a site; always paired with a depth. seam (v.): to find the boundary between two cultural layers. echo (v.): to repeat a trait at a distance. Nabta echoes Kemet would state a similarity between pastoralist ritual and early Egyptian practice. Names for the two halves Kemet-up and Kemet-low: the sets of Upper and Lower Egypt before union. Their unification is written Kemet-up ⊕ Kemet-low. Modifiers thin (adj.): evidence is sparse. dense (adj.): evidence is abundant. cross (adj.): spans two language or culture families. A claim marked cross demands extra evidence. For instance, linking a Gur-speaking group to an Afroasiatic one is a cross claim. Part 3: Sample sentences 1. Sahar · d3 · Humf · drains · Lake-set · -ta "At depth 3, the humidity function falls as lake deposits shrink (excavated)." 2. Sahar · d0 · Humf-memory · exists · Tuareg-set · -ko "Living communities hold memory of a wetter Sahara (carried knowledge)." 3. Loropen · x-d4 · Gap · tests · older-layer · -sa "Beneath Loropeni, an unexcavated fourth layer is proposed to contain older occupation (speculative)." 4. Kasen-chain · Sahar → Kassena · cross · c0.3 · -mi "A descent chain from Saharan populations to the Kassena is plausible but weak (inferred, confidence 0.3)." 5. Kassena → Kemet · cross · thin · c0.05 · -sa "A direct link from the Kassena to Egypt is a cross-family, thinly supported hypothesis." 6. Kemet-up ⊕ Kemet-low · Narmer · d-3100 · -ta "Narmer unified Upper and Lower Egypt around 3100 BCE (documented, though the process was probably gradual)." 7. Basin(Volta) · tests · Hydro · c0.2 · -sa "A survey is proposed to test hydrocarbon potential in the Volta Basin." Part 4: Three rules of good style Never drop the mark. An unmarked claim is invalid. Upgrade by digging. A claim moves from -sa to -mi to -ta only through new evidence, never through repetition. Preserve both halves. When you write A ⊕ B, you must be able to recover A and B.
